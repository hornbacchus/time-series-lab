## What It Does
A Gaussian Hidden Markov Model treats the series as generated by an unobserved (hidden) state that switches over time, where each state has its own mean and variance and transitions follow a Markov chain. It discovers regimes you did not label in advance — a calm state and a stress state, say — and returns the most likely state at each point in time. Outputs are the per-state means and variances, the transition matrix, and the decoded state path.

## When to Use It
- You believe the series moves between a few distinct regimes you cannot observe directly (you see the returns, not the regime).
- You want each regime's statistical profile (mean, variance) and the probability of moving between regimes.
- You want a decoded timeline of which regime was active when.
- Use HMM when the regime is hidden and you want a clean state segmentation; use `markov_switching` when you also want regime-dependent regression or AR dynamics, not just a mean/variance per state.

## How to Read the Result
Each state's mean and variance give it an economic identity. On the SP500 reference a two-state model finds State 0 with a positive mean (+0.109, 1,827 observations) — a calm/up regime — and State 1 with a negative mean (−0.103, 685 observations) — a stress/down regime. The transition matrix tells you persistence (how likely each regime is to continue). Two honesty checks matter: the fit is by EM, which converges to a *local* optimum that depends on initialization, so confirm the model converged and that the multiple restarts agreed (the reported log-likelihood is the best across restarts); and state labels are inherently arbitrary, so the engine fixes the ordering by sorting states by mean — read the means, not the index.

## Related Techniques
- *(use after)* `markov_switching` if you want regime-dependent regression/AR coefficients on top of the state segmentation.
- *(alternatives)* `markov_switching` (regime-switching regression/AR); `tar_setar` if the regime is driven by an observed threshold rather than a hidden state; `critical_slowing_down` for detecting an approaching transition rather than segmenting existing ones.

## Technical Detail
Estimation is a Gaussian HMM (hmmlearn `GaussianHMM`) fit by the EM/Baum-Welch algorithm with multiple random restarts (3, 5, or 10 by preset), keeping the best fit by log-likelihood, followed by Viterbi decoding of the state path. State identities are made deterministic by relabeling — states sorted by mean, or by standard deviation when a high-variance state dominates — to defeat label-switching across runs. You set the number of states and the covariance type; the iteration cap and forecast horizon default by preset.
*Reference run:* sp500_returns.csv (2,512 daily log-return %), 2 states, full covariance, Balanced (5 restarts), seed 42 — State 0 mean +0.109 (1,827 observations, calm), State 1 mean −0.103 (685 observations, stress); log-likelihood −3297.32, AIC 6608.6, converged with 5 restarts.
